# World Cup 2026: The Quarterfinals Are Done. Here's Your Final Four.

*Spain finally woke up. England didn't. Messi somehow got more Messi. And the host nation made a semifinal. Not a drill.*

---

Four teams left. Two semifinals this week. One final on July 19.

The quarterfinals delivered. Let me tell you why.

---

## How We Got Here: The QF Recap

**France 2-0 Norway** — July 3, Dallas

The tournament's feel-good story ran out of road. Norway and Haaland had been genuinely brilliant through the group stage and a nervy round of 16, but France in a proper knockout game is a different species entirely. Mbappe scored before Norway had time to settle (8th minute), Barcola added the second with 20 minutes left, and that was that. Haaland had one clear chance. One. Lloris — back from a two-year international retirement for this tournament specifically — didn't even have to dive for it.

Norway made this World Cup mean something. They'll be back in 2030 with a full squad around Haaland at peak age. That thought is terrifying and exciting in equal measure.

**Spain 3-1 England** — July 3, MetLife Stadium

England. In a World Cup quarterfinal. Against Spain. Trailing 2-0 at halftime.

Look, nobody saw this coming. Well — everyone saw this coming.

Spain were the best team in the world before the tournament and you wouldn't have known it from their group stage. The Cape Verde 0-0 created a narrative that they were vulnerable, which was true, but the reason they were vulnerable in that game had nothing to do with the Spain that showed up in the knockouts. Pedri and Gavi finding each other 47 times per half will do that. Once the engine started running in the round of 16, the rest of the bracket became a formality.

England's xG was decent in the first half. 1.3, which is fine. They had 2 shots on target in 45 minutes. Kane headed one against the crossbar. Bellingham had a moment in the 43rd minute that should have been a goal and wasn't. If either of those had gone in, it's a different game.

They didn't. It wasn't.

Ferran Torres completed his World Cup redemption arc — after three years of being the guy who looks great in training and gets subbed off at halftime — with a brace in 11 minutes. Yamal, 18 years old, set up both of them and then produced a third-goal assist that Dani Olmo finished with a calm that felt almost disrespectful.

England pulled one back through Rashford. Got excited for about 6 minutes. Then Pedri nutmegged their captain in their own box and the game was done as a competition.

**Argentina 2-1 Brazil** — July 5, SoFi Stadium

El Clásico del Río de la Plata. At a World Cup. In Los Angeles.

Brazil, for all their injury absences at the start, found form through the knockouts. Endrick — 19 years old, built like a freight train, completely incapable of being intimidated — had been electric from the round of 32 onward. Vinícius was Vinícius when Brazil needed him. The version of Brazil that showed up at SoFi was not the version that drew with Morocco on June 13.

Didn't matter.

Messi scored in the 23rd minute off a Julián Álvarez cutback that was — and I need you to understand this — his 19th World Cup goal across six tournaments. The most in history. Eusébio's record from 1966. Gone.

He didn't celebrate. He pointed at the sky, walked back to the center circle, and looked at Brazil like he was sorry for what was about to happen to them.

Brazil equalized through Endrick right before halftime (of course), and for 45 minutes of the second half it was the best game of the tournament. Alisson saved two Argentina chances that statistically should have been goals. Martínez denied Vinícius twice from inside the six-yard box. It was chaotic and athletic and completely brilliant.

Di María — 38 years old and somehow still on the Argentina bench because Scaloni refuses to let him retire — came on in the 71st minute and won the penalty that Messi converted in the 84th.

Brazil is out. Messi is in the semifinal.

**USA 1-0 Germany** — July 5, AT&T Stadium, Dallas

The loudest stadium I've seen at a World Cup since Japan-Germany in Qatar.

Germany had every right to expect this quarterfinal to go differently. They'd been clinical through the tournament — Musiala in particular was playing the best football of his career — and the USA, for all their heart and home support, had a clear ceiling compared to the Europeans they'd been drawing in the bracket.

The problem with ceiling-based analysis is that it completely ignores what 95,000 Americans do to a match environment when their team gets a set piece in the 57th minute and Christian Pulisic steps over the ball and Weston McKennie hits it into the roof of the net.

That stadium lost its mind. Genuinely. You could feel it through a screen.

Germany pushed. Musiala hit the bar in the 71st minute in a moment that will feature in every "what if" compilation for the next decade. Nagelsmann threw everyone forward in the last 15 minutes. Matt Turner — who has been the tournament's most underrated goalkeeper — made two saves in stoppage time that were legitimately world-class.

Final score: 1-0 USA. Host nation through to the semifinals.

Julian Nagelsmann's face at the final whistle looked like a man who was trying to figure out which exit leads to the parking lot in a stadium he'd never visited.

## Your Semifinal Matchups

**July 9 — France vs Spain** — Hard Rock Stadium, Miami

The final we wanted early. The tournament gods said: July 9, semifinal.

This is the best match of the tournament regardless of where it falls on the bracket. Mbappe vs Pedri. Deschamps vs de la Fuente. France's explosiveness on the counter vs Spain's suffocating midfield control.

France are still the favorites at +210. Spain have looked better through the knockouts at +280. This one goes 90 minutes at minimum and probably extra time.

My lean: France. The margin over every team in this tournament when their press activates is still elite. Spain's build-up can be disrupted in ways Norway and England couldn't manage.

**July 10 — Argentina vs USA** — MetLife Stadium, East Rutherford

The tournament final by any metric that matters.

On one side: the defending champions, the GOAT, the team that has been building for this specific tournament since July 19, 2022 in Lusail. Scaloni's Argentina knows how to close out tournaments. They've now won 3 Copa Américas and a World Cup since 2021. They don't collapse.

On the other side: 95,000 Americans who have been making MetLife Stadium the loudest venue in the competition for three consecutive knockout games, a Pulisic who is playing the football of his career, a Turner who has been unbeatable, and a home crowd that genuinely turns neutral venues hostile.

Messi is +140 to score first. That line exists because the alternative is unthinkable.

He is 38 years old. He is averaging a goal contribution every 34 minutes. He has broken the all-time World Cup scoring record with two games left to play. And he is somehow doing this while being fouled more than any player at the tournament because every opponent figures that's the only way to stop him.

They are wrong. But at least they're trying.

**Mbappe at 26 vs Messi at 38 — still the final we get:**

The bracket has conspired to put them on separate sides. If both win their semis — France over Spain, Argentina over USA — the 2022 Qatar final is getting a rematch. Same final. Same two captains. Except this time Mbappe has three fewer Champions League finals under his belt and Messi has been the best player in the world for the past month.

I wrote in June that France lifts the trophy on July 19. I'm not changing that prediction. But if Argentina and Messi get to that final, I'll have to watch it through my fingers because I'm not completely sure anymore.

Cape Verde were eliminated in the round of 16 by Spain, 2-0. Vozinha made 6 saves. Spain needed a Pedri free kick and a Torres tap-in to put them away. The 40-year-old second-division goalkeeper who shut out Spain in the group stage and made the entire football world fall in love with this tournament got a standing ovation from 70,000 people when he walked off the pitch.

## My Take

The 48-team World Cup was supposed to cheapen the competition. More teams, more mismatches, diluted quality, blah blah. Then Cape Verde held Spain scoreless. Scotland topped a group with Brazil. Norway went to a quarterfinal with Haaland having the tournament of his life. USA eliminated Germany.

The group stage *was* bloated. Some of it *was* a bit much. But the format created pathways for nations that have never had them, and once the tournament hit the knockouts it turned into pure, unfiltered chaos with the best players in the world doing things that have no business happening on a football pitch.

Messi set the all-time World Cup scoring record against Brazil at SoFi Stadium while 70,000 Brazilians watched and the rest of the world just kind of accepted that this is what his career has always been.

Two semifinal games this week. France-Spain is the tactical masterpiece. Argentina-USA is the emotional one. If we get France-Argentina in the final, I genuinely don't know what I'm going to do with the 90 minutes that follow other than probably not breathe for most of them.

July 19. Let's go.
