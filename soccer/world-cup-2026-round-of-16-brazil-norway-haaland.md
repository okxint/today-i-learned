# Haaland Just Ended Brazil's World Cup. The Round of 16 is Chaos.

*Germany: out. Brazil: out. Two matches still happening today. The 2026 World Cup is not going to plan for anyone.*

---

Today is July 6. Two Round of 16 games are happening right now — Spain vs Portugal in Dallas, USA vs Belgium in Seattle. Six games have been played. And the storylines already are absolutely insane.

Let me catch you up.

---

## What Just Happened to Brazil

July 5. Norway 2-1 Brazil.

Erling Haaland. 78th minute header. 89th minute long-range bomb.

Brazil pulled one back through a Neymar penalty deep in stoppage time but it didn't matter. Norway held on. Brazil — five-time World Champions, perennial tournament favorites, the country that treated this as a birthright — are out in the Round of 16.

The last time Brazil went out at this stage was Italia 1990. They lost to Argentina. The generation that grew up watching Romário swore it would never happen again. It's 2026 and it just happened again.

Haaland finished the game with two goals, an assist, and what looked like a very deliberate tap of his own chest when the final whistle went. He'd been talking about this moment for five years. He wasn't subtle about it.

Brazil's problem isn't this game specifically — it's the accumulation. Neymar looked like a player who's been held together with medical tape and willpower for three seasons now. Vinicius had flashes but nobody to connect with. The squad that should have been built around Rodrygo and Endrick is instead built around a 34-year-old whose hamstrings send passive aggressive messages to the coaching staff every Tuesday.

This tournament was supposed to be Vinicius's coming-out party on the world stage. He's going home in the Round of 16 instead. Not the story anyone wrote in their preview piece.

**Morocco 3-0 Canada** — not a surprise on paper, but 3-0 is a statement. Morocco's defensive organization through the group stage was the best in the tournament. Seeing them go 3-0 against a Canada side that had been decent in the groups suggests they're hitting form at exactly the right time. France-Morocco quarterfinal is going to be something.

**France 1-0 Paraguay** — boring result, uncomfortable performance. France won. Mbappe got his goal. But Paraguay were compact and disciplined and France didn't look like a team about to dismantle anyone. 1-0. Fine. Moving on.

**England 3-2 Mexico** — the match of the tournament. Red card. Two penalties. Bellingham scoring a 94th-minute winner after Mexico equalized in the 88th. Played partially in a Mexican home ground atmosphere which was extraordinary. England won the game and the Mexicans applauded them off the pitch. That basically never happens. Kane had two assists and a goal contribution. Bellingham looked like the best player at the tournament. England in a quarterfinal.

## The Germany Shock You May Have Missed

Go back to the Round of 32.

**Paraguay 4-3 (penalties) Germany.**

Germany, four-time World Champions, Musiala-Havertz-Gnabry-Nagelsmann's Germany, are out. Eliminated by Paraguay. On penalties. In the Round of 32 of a tournament they started 7-1 in the group stage.

The game was level after 120 minutes. Went to penalties. Paraguay converted all four of theirs. Germany missed two.

Julian Nagelsmann's face in that press conference should be preserved in a museum somewhere. Not because he did anything wrong — Germany played well enough to win the game over 120 minutes — but because the face of a man who cannot understand how the Paraguay goalkeeper is celebrating while his team gets on a plane home captures something true and permanent about football.

Germany being eliminated before the quarterfinals in a World Cup is its own genre of event at this point. But somehow this one felt different. Musiala in this tournament was electric. They should have gone further. That's football.

## The Golden Boot Race — Three-Way Tie at 7 Goals

Going into the quarterfinals:

| Player | Country | Goals |
|--------|---------|-------|
| Kylian Mbappe | France | **7** |
| Erling Haaland | Norway | **7** |
| Lionel Messi | Argentina | **7** |

That's the three best players in the world — separated by nothing but assists and minutes played — heading into the quarterfinals.

Mbappe is 26. Haaland is 25. Messi is 38.

Messi has scored 7 World Cup goals in this tournament alone. He's at 19 total across his career. Klose's all-time record was 16. That record is gone. He broke it sometime around the Round of 16 and barely seemed to notice.

One of these three is going to win the Golden Boot. Any outcome feels correct and absurd simultaneously.

**Norway vs England** is the quarter I'm most curious about. Haaland is the best striker left in the tournament. England's defense has been consistently not great. This has 3-2 written all over it and I mean that for either team.

**Argentina's quarter** (vs whoever comes through the lower bracket) is the one everyone is treating as a walkover. Probably isn't.

## Morocco's Quietly Brilliant Tournament

Nobody is talking about this enough.

Morocco have:
- Shut out Canada 3-0 in the R16
- Knocked out the Netherlands on penalties in the Round of 32
- Let in one goal in their last four games combined

Their defensive setup under Regragui is the best-organized thing in this tournament. Center-back pairing is immovable. Midfield presses from the front. Ziyech when he's healthy is still capable of creating something from nothing.

France-Morocco on July 9 in Boston is the game I'm most afraid to predict. France have the individual quality advantage. Morocco have the defensive discipline and a crowd that was somehow louder than any American crowd despite being the away team in every game so far.

2022 semifinals were France 2-0 Morocco. I'm not picking a repeat.

## My Take

When Germany got knocked out by Paraguay in the Round of 32, everyone thought "okay, upset of the tournament, moving on." Then Brazil went out to Norway and suddenly the tournament looks completely different.

The path is wide open. France are still the most talented team. Argentina with Messi in this form is terrifying. But Morocco reaching a quarterfinal against France, and Norway-England being a legitimate coin flip — this is exactly what the 48-team format was supposed to produce. Not 7-1 scorelines, but genuine unpredictability.

Haaland-Mbappe-Messi at 7 goals each with multiple knockout games to go is the best sporting storyline I've seen in years.
