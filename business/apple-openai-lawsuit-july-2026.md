# Apple dumped OpenAI, then sued them. The lawsuit reads like a corporate spy thriller.

**Date:** 2026-07-18
**Topic:** Business / AI / Product

---

The Apple-OpenAI breakup was already messy. One company had the world's most valuable hardware brand. The other had the most powerful AI. They shook hands, put ChatGPT inside Siri, announced it on a WWDC stage with Sam Altman in the front row, and made it look like a clean partnership.

Then Apple quietly switched from OpenAI to Google Gemini for the next version of Apple Intelligence. No drama, no public statement, just a partnership that faded.

Now Apple has filed a federal lawsuit in Northern California alleging that OpenAI systematically stole Apple's hardware trade secrets, and the filing reads like a corporate espionage novel.

## The cast

**Tang Yew Tan** spent 25 years at Apple. He oversaw product design for the iPhone and the Apple Watch. He left in March 2024 and became OpenAI's Chief Hardware Officer. Apple's lawsuit says that in his new role, he directed job candidates still working at Apple to bring physical hardware components to their OpenAI interviews for "show and tell" sessions — so that Tang and his team could extract proprietary information.

Apple also alleges Tang coached new hires to not tell Apple they were leaving for OpenAI, so they could remain at Apple as long as possible and continue funneling information out.

**Chang Liu** spent eight years at Apple as a senior electrical engineer. When he left for OpenAI in 2026, he didn't return his Apple-issued laptop. According to the filing, he used it to download confidential technical documents after leaving. A bug in Apple's cloud storage system allowed him to continue accessing Apple files while already employed at OpenAI.

**Jony Ive's io Products** is named in the complaint. The hardware design firm that OpenAI acquired last year for $6.5 billion — the one that everyone assumed was Jony Ive's comeback vehicle — is accused of using a metal-finishing technique that Apple invented, while "misleading" manufacturing partners into thinking they had Apple's permission to use it.

The complaint notes that over 400 former Apple employees now work at OpenAI.

## The context that makes this stranger

The part that makes this genuinely weird is the timeline. Apple had an active partnership with OpenAI. ChatGPT was integrated into iOS 18. Sam Altman was on an Apple stage. All while, according to Apple, Tang Tan was running recruiting operations that extracted confidential hardware information from Apple employees who were in the middle of active interviews.

Then Apple pivoted to Gemini. The official explanation was that Gemini was the better fit for Apple Intelligence going forward. The unofficial read, now that this lawsuit has landed: Apple may have been watching what was happening with its own employees and decided the OpenAI partnership was a liability.

Apple didn't sue OpenAI immediately after parting ways. They waited, watched, and filed a federal case alleging a scheme that operated "at every level" of OpenAI's hardware operation.

## What OpenAI is actually building

This explains why the lawsuit matters more than a typical corporate IP dispute. OpenAI acquired io Products — Jony Ive's firm — for $6.5 billion because it wants to be in hardware. The company that trained the world's best AI models now wants to make a device that runs it. That puts OpenAI in direct competition with Apple for the first time, not just as an AI provider but as a consumer hardware maker.

Apple suing OpenAI for hardware trade secrets, right as OpenAI is trying to build its first hardware product with the guy who designed every meaningful Apple device of the last three decades, is not a coincidence. It's Apple saying: we know exactly what you're doing, and we're going to make it expensive.

The discovery process alone is going to be damaging for OpenAI. Even if the lawsuit settles, the depositions, the document requests, and the internal emails are going to surface things that OpenAI's hardware ambitions don't need showing up in a federal court filing.

## My take

The Apple-OpenAI story has always been underreported as a pure product story. Apple needed AI fast and picked the best available partner. OpenAI needed Apple's billion-plus device install base and picked the best available distribution. It was a business transaction, not a friendship.

What the lawsuit reveals is that both parties were playing different games simultaneously. Apple was running a partner relationship while apparently tracking what its own employees were doing at OpenAI. OpenAI was building a partnership with Apple while its hardware chief was allegedly using that proximity to accelerate its own hardware roadmap.

The Gemini switch and the lawsuit together make more sense than either does alone. Apple didn't just find a better AI — they decided the OpenAI relationship had become a strategic liability and found a way to exit on their own terms while taking legal action on the way out.

For anyone building products at the intersection of AI and hardware, this case is worth watching. It's going to define what "partnership" means when the companies involved are building toward the same end state.
